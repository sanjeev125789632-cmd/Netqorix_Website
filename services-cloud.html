01 / Who this is for

For businesses whose website or app needs somewhere reliable to live.

Every website and application runs on a computer somewhere. "The cloud" simply means renting that computer from a provider such as Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure or DigitalOcean, instead of buying and running a server yourself.

This page is for businesses that need that set up properly the first time, or need someone to look after it afterwards. If you already have a working website on basic shared hosting and it is coping fine, you may not need this page at all.

02 / Cloud explained in everyday terms

Renting a shop, not buying the building.

Buying a server is like buying a building

You pay a large amount up front, you own it, and you are responsible for the electricity, the locks, the repairs and the fact that it is the wrong size in two years.

Cloud is like renting a shop unit

You pay monthly for the space you use. If business grows, you take a bigger unit. If it shrinks, you take a smaller one. You are not stuck with hardware you no longer need.

You still pay the landlord and the shopfitter separately

The cloud provider is the landlord and bills you for usage. Netqorix is the shopfitter and the manager, and bills for the work of designing, building and running the setup. These are two different bills, and your quote shows both.

The meter runs on usage, not on a fixed plan

Cloud providers bill for what you actually consume: server time, storage, data transfer, database hours. A quiet month costs less than a busy one. This is why a cloud bill is an estimate until real traffic exists.

03 / Problems this can address

When basic hosting stops being enough.

  • The site slows down or falls over when traffic rises. A campaign, a festival season or press coverage brings more visitors than the current hosting can handle.
  • There are no real backups. Nobody can say when the last backup was taken or whether it can actually be restored.
  • Deployment is manual and risky. Files are dragged onto a server by hand, and every release is a gamble.
  • Nobody knows who has access. Passwords are shared, there are no separate accounts, and ex-staff or ex-vendors may still have keys.
  • The bill is unpredictable. Cloud charges arrive with no explanation of what drove them.
  • There is no test environment. Every change is made directly on the live site.

04 / How it works

What setup and management actually involve.

Setup is a one-time piece of engineering

Choosing the provider, sizing the servers, configuring the network and firewall rules, setting up access control so each person has their own account with only the permissions they need, installing SSL, connecting the database, and building a deployment pipeline so releases are repeatable instead of manual.

Management is an ongoing arrangement

Watching that everything is up, taking and testing backups, applying security patches, adjusting capacity when traffic changes, renewing certificates, and reporting on what the infrastructure is doing and costing.

Provider choice is decided by your workload

Netqorix works with AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and DigitalOcean. The right one depends on your application, expected traffic, who needs to operate it and your budget — not on a blanket preference. A small business website and a high-traffic application deserve different answers.

06 / How cloud billing works

Two bills, and which one is which.

Bill one: the cloud provider

AWS, Google Cloud, Azure or DigitalOcean charge for what your application consumes — server time, storage, data transfer, managed database hours, and any extra services. This is metered and varies month to month with your actual usage. Netqorix does not set these prices and cannot fix them in advance.

Bill two: Netqorix

The one-time setup fee, and the monthly managed plan fee if you take one. These are Netqorix rates and are fixed in your quote.

When Netqorix holds the account for you

If the cloud account sits under Netqorix management, provider usage is passed on at direct cost plus a 15–20% management margin. You see the underlying provider cost and the margin as separate lines.

When you hold the account yourself

The provider bills your card or your business directly, and there is no markup because Netqorix is not paying that bill. You pay only the setup and managed plan fees. Many businesses prefer this because it keeps ownership of the account unambiguous.

Ask for both bills to be itemised before you approve any cloud quote. A number that combines Netqorix fees with an estimate of provider usage is not something you can budget against.

08 / What this looks like in practice

Two situations, described as hypotheticals.

These are illustrative scenarios written to explain how the tiers differ. They are not Netqorix client projects.

Hypothetical example

A five-page business website with a contact form. Low traffic, no logins, nothing to process. This can run on a small static or serverless setup. A one-time setup at the lower end of the band plus a Starter managed plan at ₹4,000 / mo is usually more than enough, and the provider bill itself will be small.

Hypothetical example

A booking application with accounts, payments and busy weekends. Traffic spikes, a database that must not lose data, and downtime that costs real bookings. This needs autoscaling, a managed database, alerting and a tested restore procedure — a Business managed plan, with the provider bill rising and falling with booking volume.

09 / The delivery process

How a cloud engagement runs.

  1. Share what you are running

    Tell us what the application is, roughly how much traffic it handles, where it runs now, and whether anything has already failed.

  2. Review and recommendation

    Sanjeev reviews the workload and recommends a provider and a setup sized to it, with the reason stated rather than assumed.

  3. Fixed quote

    You receive the one-time setup fee, the recommended managed plan if any, an estimate of provider usage with its assumptions stated, and who will hold the account.

  4. Build and migrate

    The environment is built, the application is deployed to it, backups and monitoring are switched on, and the setup is tested before anything is cut over.

Migration time depends on the size of the existing setup, the data involved, and DNS propagation, which no provider controls. A schedule is confirmed in your quote rather than promised in advance on this page.

Frequently asked questions

Cloud and infrastructure questions.

What does 'cloud' actually mean for my business?

It means renting computing power from a provider such as AWS, Google Cloud, Azure or DigitalOcean instead of buying and running your own server. You pay for what you use, you can grow or shrink the setup as needed, and the provider looks after the physical hardware.

Do I have to use cloud hosting?

No. A small, low-traffic business website often runs perfectly well on ordinary shared hosting at a much lower cost. Cloud makes sense when you need to handle variable traffic, run an application with a database, keep separate test and live environments, or control who has access to what.

Is the cloud provider's bill included in your monthly fee?

No. The managed plan fee is what Netqorix charges for the work of running your infrastructure. AWS, GCP, Azure or DigitalOcean bill separately for the resources you consume. If the account is held under Netqorix management, provider usage is passed on at direct cost plus a 15–20% management margin, shown as separate lines.

Can I hold the cloud account in my own name?

Yes, and many businesses should. You pay the provider directly, there is no management margin because Netqorix is not paying that bill, and ownership of the account is unambiguous. Netqorix sets it up, hands over the access and can still manage it under a monthly plan.

How much will my cloud bill be?

It depends on real usage, which nobody can know precisely before launch. Your quote includes an estimate with the assumptions stated — expected traffic, storage and database size. Actual charges will move with actual usage, and the first two or three months are the honest test.

Which provider do you recommend?

It depends on the application, expected workload, who needs to operate it and your budget. Netqorix works with AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and DigitalOcean and gives a reason for the recommendation rather than defaulting to one provider for every client.

Can you guarantee 100% uptime?

No. Cloud providers themselves publish availability targets rather than guarantees. Monitoring means a failure is detected and acted on quickly, and tested backups mean it can be recovered. Netqorix does not publish an uptime guarantee it does not control.

What happens if I want to leave?

The account and its access are yours. Documentation and credentials are handed over, and you can move to another provider or manage it in-house. There is no lock-in mechanism designed to make leaving difficult.
